Tobacco Wars

A century of fictional marketing battles, read against the real 2022 filings of Philip Morris International, BAT, and Imperial Brands — three different playbooks for the same shift to reduced-risk products.

Case: Tobacco Wars (fictional) Benchmarked: PMI FY2022 10-K Benchmarked: BAT FY2022 Form 20-F Benchmarked: Imperial Brands FY2022 Annual Report
01

The case, in one line

A century of pseudonymous "Big Smoke" and "Tobacco Empire" brands moving from doctor-endorsed cigarette ads, through the 1964 Surgeon General's report and the 1998 Master Settlement Agreement, to today's smokeless pivot — e-cigarettes, snus, heat-not-burn, and nicotine pouches marketed as harm reduction.

The case ends on an open question: as regulation tightens and consumer preference shifts, what does it actually take for a tobacco company to keep its market position? The real 2022 filings of the three largest listed players answer that question three different ways.

03

Three strategic archetypes for the shift era

Same industry pressure, same year — three entirely different bets, each sized to the company's own scale and balance sheet.

Transform

Philip Morris International

PMI has redirected almost the entire company toward smoke-free products: IQOS is sold in 73 markets with 24.9 million users, roughly 71% of whom are estimated to have fully switched off cigarettes. Since 2008, PMI has invested over $10.5 billion in smoke-free R&D and manufacturing — and in 2022 that category alone reached 99% of total R&D spend.

Smoke-free revenue: 32.1% of totalIQOS users: 24.9M (+3.2M)Cumulative investment: >$10.5bn

Scale, deliberately

British American Tobacco

BAT is growing New Categories fast (+40.9% revenue) while funding it internally: its "Quantum" efficiency programme delivered £1.9bn in annualised cost savings since 2020, letting BAT hit its 2025 New Category profitability target a full year early — all while still growing its dividend 6.0% and returning £6.9bn to shareholders.

New Category revenue: +40.9%Profitability target: 1 year earlyShareholder returns: £6.9bn

Harvest and return capital

Imperial Brands

Imperial describes itself in its own filing as "the smallest of the four global tobacco players" and has chosen not to chase RRP scale at all — NGP R&D isn't even separately disclosed. Instead it concentrates on five priority combustible markets (US, Germany, UK, Spain, Australia, ~70% of operating profit) — and actually gained cigarette share in the US (+90bps), UK (+85bps), and Australia (+20bps) in 2022, while funding a £1bn buyback plus a growing dividend.

04

The regulatory squeeze now hits the substitutes too

The case frames regulation as something the industry has always adapted around. The 2022 filings show that adaptation is getting harder precisely where the industry pinned its future: the reduced-risk category itself.

Same year, same regulator, two companies

The FDA issued Marketing Denial Orders against menthol variants of BAT's Vuse Solo/Vibe/Ciro (Jan 2023) and against Imperial's myblu (April 2022) within months of each other — both companies are appealing. California separately banned flavoured tobacco products in December 2022.

Litigation risk hasn't gone away

PMI disclosed a Canadian class-action judgment of roughly CAD 15.5 billion in damages (Blais/Létourneau), with its Canadian subsidiary under bankruptcy protection. BAT booked £450m in charges tied to US DOJ/OFAC sanctions investigations, on top of £612m from exiting Russia and Belarus.

05

What "keeping market share" actually requires

  1. Size the transformation bet to the balance sheet, not to the trend.PMI can direct 99% of R&D to smoke-free because its combustible cash flow is large enough to fund it without starving shareholders. A company Imperial's size chasing the same ratio would likely fund neither transformation nor returns well — which is exactly why Imperial doesn't try.
  2. Fund the new category out of efficiency, not just growth.BAT's route to hitting its profitability target early wasn't more revenue alone — it was £1.9bn of internally generated savings freeing up reinvestment without diluting shareholder returns.
  3. Defending a legacy category is a legitimate strategy, not a failure to transform.Imperial's share gains in the US, UK, and Australia show disciplined focus on fewer markets can outperform spreading thin across every emerging category.
  4. Expect the regulatory fight to follow the product, not stay with cigarettes.Two of three companies took direct FDA enforcement action against their flagship reduced-risk products in the same year — the "safer alternative" positioning doesn't exempt a product from scrutiny, it just changes which regulator shows up.
Bottom line — there is no single "correct" response to the shift toward substitutes. The efficient move is choosing, deliberately, which of the three plays your company's revenue base, cost structure, and regulatory exposure actually support — then funding it without compromising the balance sheet that makes it possible.
