Cola Wars

ColaNova vs. PopFizz — a fictitious rivalry benchmarked against the real FY2022 results of Coca-Cola and PepsiCo, to find out how the actual two largest beverage companies compete.

How this was built. ColaNova and PopFizz, their founders, and their brand histories are entirely fictitious, as specified in the assignment brief, and are not connected to any real company or person. Every figure under The Real Rivalry, The Strategic Divide, and Sustainability as Competitive Terrain below is drawn directly from The Coca-Cola Company's 2022 Business & Sustainability Report and PepsiCo's 2022 Annual Report — the real "two largest cola companies" the assignment asks to analyse. Figures are cited inline; full sourcing is listed in the footer.
The case

Two strategies wearing one industry's clothes

ColaNova and PopFizz look, on paper, like a straightforward rivalry: one legacy cola brand defending nostalgia and emotional connection, one insurgent defending wellness and sustainability. That framing is a useful teaching device, but it undersells what's actually interesting about the real cola industry. The two real companies large enough to matter here aren't really fighting each other over who makes the better-tasting cola anymore — they're running two different bets about what kind of company a beverage company should be. That distinction is the most useful thing this case can teach, so it's where the real-world benchmark below is aimed.

ColaNova

Legacy, breadth, emotional brand equity
  • Flagship "Elixir Refresh," heritage positioning across generations
  • Vertical integration into supply chain and bottling
  • Limited-edition, culturally-tied flavor drops
  • Localized R&D centers for regional taste profiles

PopFizz

Wellness, diversification, sustainability
  • Naturally sweetened lines, functional beverages
  • Brand extension into healthy snacks and wellness goods
  • Acquired a premium natural-juice company
  • Eco-friendly packaging, renewable-energy production

The strategic divide

Focus vs. diversification — not legacy vs. wellness

The single most useful data point in either report is PepsiCo's own revenue mix. It is not a beverage company that happens to sell some snacks — it is, by revenue, a food company:

PepsiCo — mix of net revenue, FY2022
Food 58% Beverage 42%
PepsiCo — core division operating profit mix, FY2022
Frito-Lay N.A. 44% Beverages N.A. 19% Other divisions 37%

Frito-Lay North America alone contributes 44% of PepsiCo's core division operating profit — more than PepsiCo's entire North American beverage business. Coca-Cola, by contrast, has stayed almost entirely inside beverages, competing instead through brand breadth (200+ brands) and an asset-light franchise model — Coca-Cola owns the concentrate and the brand; independent bottling partners own most of the capital-heavy production and distribution.

The insight worth taking into the case These are two different answers to the same risk: what happens when cola-specific demand softens (sugar taxes, health trends, changing snacking habits)? PepsiCo's answer was to diversify the revenue base into an adjacent category it could dominate. Coca-Cola's answer was to stay focused and make the beverage portfolio itself more resilient — reformulating flagship products, expanding into water, tea, coffee and energy under one franchise system, and letting bottling partners absorb capital risk. Both are coherent strategies. Neither is "the safe one."
Sustainability as competitive terrain

ESG is no longer PopFizz's differentiator — it's table stakes

The case treats sustainability as PopFizz's distinct positioning against ColaNova's tradition. In the real market, both real companies now run comparably serious, publicly-reported sustainability programs — because regulators, retailers and investors now expect it of scale players in this category, not just of the wellness-positioned challenger.

For ColaNova and PopFizz, the implication is uncomfortable for the fictional insurgent: sustainability credentials alone won't hold as a durable moat, because the legacy incumbent can and will match the commitment at scale. The moat has to come from something an incumbent can't as easily copy — which is closer to portfolio architecture than to messaging.

Ways of competition

Strategic recommendations

01

Pick a lane on focus vs. diversification — deliberately

ColaNova should decide, explicitly, whether it is defending a beverage-only franchise model (Coca-Cola's real bet) or diversifying into adjacent categories to de-risk cola-specific demand (PepsiCo's real bet). The case shows ColaNova drifting toward vertical integration without settling this question — that ambiguity is itself a strategic risk.

02

Treat sustainability as a cost of entry, not a differentiator

PopFizz's wellness positioning will erode as ColaNova matches packaging and reformulation commitments — which the real Coca-Cola data shows a legacy incumbent can do at scale. PopFizz needs a second, harder-to-copy advantage: likely its acquisition-driven category expertise (natural juice, functional beverages) rather than its sustainability messaging alone.

03

Decouple the pricing war from the brand war

The case's early pricing-and-marketing skirmishes are a race to the bottom that erodes margin for both companies without changing category demand. Both should compete on category expansion — new occasions, new formats, new geographies — rather than share-shifting within a flat category.

04

Build responsible-marketing guardrails before regulation forces them

Both companies' plans to market to children need explicit, published guidelines now — the real industry precedent (regulatory pressure on sugary drinks cited as a threat in both SWOTs) shows this becomes mandatory eventually. Committing early converts a future compliance cost into a present trust asset.

Reflection & discussion

Open questions

  • Focus vs. diversificationIf ColaNova diversifies the way PepsiCo really did — into an unrelated category like snacks — does that dilute or strengthen the "joy and togetherness" brand identity the case says is its core asset?
  • Copyable moatsNow that sustainability is achievable by any well-capitalized competitor, what would actually be hard for ColaNova to copy from PopFizz — and is it big enough to build a strategy on?
  • Margin vs. shareCoca-Cola's asset-light franchise model and PepsiCo's owned-manufacturing food business carry very different capital intensity. Which structure would better suit PopFizz as it scales the acquired juice business?
